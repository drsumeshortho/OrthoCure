What happens in the consultation

You do not have to know the diagnosis before you come.

The consultation is where the problem is understood step by step.

01

Listen

What brought you here, when it started, and how it is affecting daily life.

02

Examine

A clinical examination helps narrow down the likely source of the problem.

03

Review

Reports and scans are considered in the context of your symptoms and examination.

04

Explain

We discuss what the findings mean, what they do not mean, and what options exist.

05

Plan

Then we decide on a reasonable next step, including monitoring when appropriate.

A common concern

Do I need to decide about surgery at my first visit?

No. You do not need to make a decision about surgery before you understand the problem.

The first consultation is an opportunity to understand what is happening, what your options are, and what the reasonable next step is. Surgery is considered when it is clinically appropriate.

At OrthoCure Treatment decisions are made step by step. Conservative options are considered when appropriate, and investigations or surgery are used when they genuinely help change management.
Questions worth bringing

You are allowed to ask “why?”

If something is unclear, ask. A useful consultation is not just about receiving a diagnosis — it is about understanding the plan.

“What do you think is causing my symptoms?”

“Do I need any further tests?”

“What are my treatment options?”

“Can this be managed without surgery?”

“What should I do next?”

“What should I watch for?”

“Don’t come with just your scan. Come with your story.”

Your symptoms, function and experience matter alongside your reports.

Second opinions

If you are unsure, bring the information you already have.

A second opinion can be useful when you want to understand a diagnosis, treatment options or the next step more clearly.

Bring your X-rays or MRI images, written reports, prescriptions, previous treatment details and any advice you have already received. The aim is not to automatically disagree with another recommendation. It is to understand the diagnosis and options in context.

Frequently asked questions

A few practical answers

Do I need a referral to consult Dr Sumesh?
A referral is not generally required for a direct consultation. If you are unsure, you can call the clinic before your visit.
Do I need an MRI before seeing an orthopaedic doctor?
Not necessarily. The need for an X-ray, MRI, blood test or other investigation depends on your symptoms and clinical examination. You can read more about the clinical approach.
Should I bring old reports?
Yes. Old reports, scans, prescriptions and previous treatment records can be useful, especially when the problem has been present for some time.
Do I need to decide about surgery before my first consultation?
No. The first consultation is an opportunity to understand the problem, discuss appropriate options and decide on a reasonable next step.
Can I bring an MRI or X-ray for a second opinion?
Yes. Bring the images, written reports, prescriptions and any previous advice you have. You can also visit the Second Opinion page.
